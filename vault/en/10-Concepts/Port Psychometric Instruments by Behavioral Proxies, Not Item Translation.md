## Summary

When porting a human self-report questionnaire to subjects that **cannot self-report** — pets, infants — don't translate the items. Identify the psychological construct each item was measuring, then re-express it as **third-person observable behavioral proxies**.

## Details

- The failure pattern: turning "I often feel anxious" into "My dog often feels anxious" — that's now asking for the owner's guess, silently changing the measurement target from "the dog's anxiety" to "the owner's perception".
- Correct porting moves the **construct, not the item surface**:
  1. Identify the construct the original item measured (e.g. separation anxiety)
  2. List observable behaviors through which that construct manifests in the target (pacing at the door, excessive barking, destructive behavior)
  3. Rewrite as third-person observational items — "How many times last week did you observe pacing at the door?"
- Validation required: the converted scale does **not** inherit the original's validity — treat it as a new instrument and re-establish reliability and validity.
- Generalization: this is the rule for moving any measurement instrument across domains, subjects, or cultures — what you port is the measurement intent, not the wording.
