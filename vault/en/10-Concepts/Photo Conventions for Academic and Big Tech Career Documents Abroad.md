## Summary

For career materials aimed at academia or big tech abroad, the norm is a **naturally lit environmental headshot on your personal website, and no photo at all on your CV/resume PDF** — a Korean-style studio ID photo tends to backfire.

## Details

- **Website**: having a photo is standard, but style matters —
  - Recommended: an environmental headshot (natural light, upper body, everyday-to-business-casual attire, a lab or outdoor background)
  - Avoid: a formal ID-photo style (white background, suit, neutral expression) — it reads as stiff and out of place in Western contexts
- **CV/resume PDF**: the norm is no photo. In the US and elsewhere, some application systems even filter out resumes with photos to sidestep discrimination concerns.
- The cultural core of the difference: Korea and parts of Europe treat a photo as serving "verification" (confirming identity), while the Anglophone world treats it as serving "branding" (showing who you are). The same photo can build trust or undercut it depending on context.
- Application: the safest approach is to look at the websites of a few recent hires or current members in the ecosystem you're applying to and follow their norms.
