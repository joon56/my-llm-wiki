## Summary

If everyone brings a different idea and the meeting tries to merge them, it drifts — **finish collecting ideas before the meeting; in the meeting, pin down constraints and criteria first, then choose and sequence.**

## Details

- Failure scene: in a roadmap meeting, every proposal was different. The facilitator felt they were "all fine" and held back so their own (most influential) voice would not decide it -> time dragged on with no conclusion.
- Diagnosis: the meeting was not short of opinions; it **had no criteria for choosing**. "They're all fine" means there is no yardstick. Different ideas do not merge — a compromise satisfies neither side.
- Four things the facilitator holds even while staying out of the content: (1) constraints as numbers — "3-4 programs this term, zero budget, 4-5 people to run them"; once the slots are fixed, "all fine" stops being possible. (2) agree on criteria before choosing — prep burden, how many can join, whether someone owns it. (3) **drop any proposal with no owner** — the real constraint is owners, not ideas. (4) a default plus a time box — "if we can't decide in 20 minutes, we go with my draft."
- Withholding your opinion is not the same as handing off the decision: if you step out of the content, supply criteria, constraints and a deadline instead. Without both, nobody has the authority to conclude.
- Sequence instead of eliminate: sorting into "this term / next term / on hold" discards nothing, so contributors do not feel rejected.
- On a split, pilot or sequence instead of compromising: "try it this term and revisit", "A first, then B depending on response". Reversible decisions draw less opposition.
- Shape of a decision: decision + one-line rationale + owner + deadline. Without an owner and a deadline, it is not a decision.
- When minutes come in, audit them against that shape. Only one item had an owner and a date; all six review items lacked "who, by when", and "next steps" read "none" — flagged right away as a sure way to still be "under review" at the next meeting. Items with an ambiguous target (a program they attend, or a program they run?) get pinned down with a question too.
