## Summary

"Any thoughts?" and "What would you like to do?" ask people to invent ideas on the spot, so all you get back is "sounds good" or silence — **give a skeleton or options first and collect reactions (verify, push back, choose).**

## Details

- Observation: in meetings, asking "any questions or opinions?" after every point always got "sounds good". A blank survey asking "what programs would you like?" got almost no answers.
- Cause: generating is expensive — unprepared people have nothing to build from. When the facilitator talks first and most, everyone else becomes an audience, and later speakers anchor on earlier ones.
- Fix 1 - make it reactive: the facilitator shows a skeleton first and asks "agree? disagree?". Offering your own draft as something to criticize ("tell me straight if you don't like it") gets people talking.
- Fix 2 - choice questions with a named person: "Which do you prefer, option 1 or 2?" Accept "I don't know" as an answer and do not press — pressing makes people quieter next time.
- Fix 3 - silent writing: 3-5 minutes of individual writing -> list without debate -> vote. Quiet people form their own view and are not swept along by whoever spoke first.
- Fix 4 - frame written input too: even a free-form reflection gets three slots (overall take / what worries you / what you will do), an explicit "none is a valid answer", and a deadline.
- Same for feedback: replace "what do you think?" with a narrowed question ("is it lack of interest, or lack of a role?").
- Principle: a few people (facilitator, core members) generate; the many converge by reacting.
